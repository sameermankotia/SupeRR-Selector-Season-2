03 — How we defined pressure

Pressure is leverage × urgency — and it is not the same for both sides

The brief left the definition open. Ours rests on one idea most approaches miss.

Leverage

A win-probability model trained on this data measures how far one ball can swing the result, across every outcome it could produce. A death over in a dead rubber scores near zero — which is the point.

Urgency

The squeeze a side is actually under: required rate above par, wickets lost beyond the norm, the innings endgame. Signals compound rather than simply adding.

The asymmetry

A chase needing 15 an over is desperate for the batter and comfortable for the bowler. One shared pressure flag gets one group backwards, so we compute urgency twice.

04 — Does the definition hold up?

We tested it before trusting it

A pressure definition that isn't validated is an opinion. One finding surprised us: raw strike rate is the wrong test for batting pressure — under a squeeze batters attempt more, so scoring rate rises even as the situation gets harder. The honest measures are risk-adjusted.

The strongest test: each player against himself

Comparing a player with his own non-pressure baseline removes every quality confound. Among players with 120+ deliveries in both buckets, 66% of batters lose balls-per-dismissal under pressure (Wilcoxon p = 8.6×10⁻⁴) and 77% of bowlers concede more (p = 1.4×10⁻¹¹). Pressure is doing something measurable to nearly everyone.

07 — Reliability & limits

What we'd tell the room before they spend

Most of what looks like "clutch" is noise. The model is built to admit that rather than hide it, and these numbers are measured, not asserted.

How repeatable is the ranking?
Split the matches in half at random, 60 times, and correlate

Reliability near 0.37 is modest, and we want to be straight about it: the top of each list is trustworthy, the tail is closer to a coin toss. That's a property of clutch performance, not a defect of this model — so we shrink hard and publish intervals.

Where the model is least certain
  • The wicket signal is weak. Dismissals under pressure are close to irreducible noise, so ratings lean on scoring above expectation.
  • First innings is the softer half. Win probability while setting a total is far harder to model than a chase.
  • Ranks 5–10 overlap heavily. Read them as a comparable set, not a strict order — the P(top 10) figure is the honest summary.
  • No venue or pitch adjustment. Par is modelled per competition-season, not per ground.
  • Availability is out of scope. These are the best pressure performers in the data, not a prediction of who reaches the auction.
